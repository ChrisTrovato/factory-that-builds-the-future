CREATOR-OWNED REFERENCES AND WHAT TO LEARN FROM THEM
These are observed presentation and launch patterns, not a dataset proving virality. No engagement totals, conversion rates, or guaranteed reach are claimed. Original X launch posts were not reliably retrievable, so no X performance conclusions are drawn.

1. Bartosz Ciechanowski — Gears
https://ciechanow.ski/gears/
Observed: the page begins with moving mechanical parts, then gives readers a speed slider and builds toward the underlying relationships.
Launch lesson (our inference): show one physical consequence immediately. A real arm or gear interaction can carry the first frame; a long description of the platform can wait.

2. Neal Agarwal — Space Elevator
https://neal.fun/space-elevator/
Observed: the project presents an upward journey through the atmosphere, with an explicit scrolling action and discoveries along the route.
Launch lesson (our inference): a clear destination makes a large subject approachable. The Factory's five worlds can invite exploration before asking someone to choose a chapter.

3. Nicky Case — The Evolution of Trust, creator announcement and sharing text
https://blog.ncase.me/the-evolution-of-trust-is-out/
https://ncase.me/trust/
https://github.com/ncase/trust/blob/gh-pages/index.html
Observed: the creator's announcement names a playable guide and links directly to it. The project's own sharing text leads with a recognizable human problem, then offers an interactive explanation. The blog acknowledges that its displayed date was backdated, so it is not used to establish an exact launch day.
Launch lesson (our inference): connect an abstract subject to an existing concern. For The Factory, that concern is the gap between coursework and the machines someone wants to understand or build.

4. Red Blob Games — A* and a creator release writeup
https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html
https://www.redblobgames.com/blog/2026-08-08-differential-heuristics/
Observed: the A* introduction immediately invites moving the start and endpoint to change a route. The creator's August 2026 release writeup explains how revised visualizations made a difficult idea clearer and identifies remaining room for improvement.
Launch lesson (our inference): make the input-to-result relationship visible and keep claims bounded. A small, honest demonstration is a stronger proof of usefulness than calling the whole platform revolutionary.
